The Criticisms, Priced Out

1. Efficiency: The e-5008 Isn't as Efficient as Reviewers Wish

The complaint

Every review notes the e-5008's efficiency: "not the most efficient EV" (Autocar), "efficiency struggles at motorway speeds" (Top Gear). Real-world testing shows 3.0–3.4 mi/kWh in independent road tests, vs. Peugeot's official 3.5 mi/kWh rating for the 73 kWh model.

What it costs you

On a 5,000-mile annual allowance at 7p/kWh night charging:

  • At Peugeot's claimed efficiency (3.5 mi/kWh): 1,429 kWh/year = £100/year = £8.33/month
  • At real-world test average (3.3 mi/kWh): 1,515 kWh/year = £106/year = £8.84/month
  • The penalty for reality: ~£0.50/month, or ~£12 over the two-year lease

Verdict: Negligible. The gap between Peugeot's claim and real-world testing is trivial on a home tariff—about the cost of a chocolate bar per month. The real penalty emerges only if you motorway-commute and rely on public rapid charging (see "Who This Is For" below).

2. Real-World Range: 200–240 Miles in Winter vs. 309 Claimed

The complaint

The e-5008 claims 309 miles on the WLTP cycle. Real-world range in cool weather: 200–240 miles (reviews report ~232 miles in a moderate-temp test). Motorway driving drops efficiency further.

What it costs you

On your home tariff: nothing. You charge at night and wake to a full battery. The penalty appears only if you rely on public rapid charging for long trips:

  • 200-mile real-world range = 2 rapid-charging stops per 400-mile trip
  • At 30p/kWh average rapid-charging cost (DC, post-network premium): ~£40 per 200-mile charge
  • Claimed range = 1 rapid-charging stop per 400 miles = £40 charge
  • No extra cost because you're still making one rapid charge per leg

Verdict: Free on home charging (which is why the 5k-mile annual limit works). On motorway trips with paid public charging, the difference between 200 and 309 miles is real, but the e-5008's fast 30-minute 10–80% charge keeps you moving. It matters emotionally far more than financially.

3. Lack of Physical Climate Controls

The complaint

The e-5008 relies entirely on the touchscreen for temperature, ventilation, and seat heating. Some reviewers found this fiddly, especially on the move.

What it costs you

Zero. This is a convenience complaint, not an economic one. If you dislike touchscreen-only climate, you'll grind your teeth—but your wallet won't notice.

Verdict: Pure preference. Five years of industry trend suggests you'll adapt faster than automakers revert to physical buttons.

4. The Dual-Motor Version Isn't Efficient Enough for 325 hp

The complaint

Peugeot also sells the e-5008 as a dual-motor 325 hp variant (157 kW front + 112 kW rear). Reviewers: "doesn't make much sense; the efficiency hit isn't worth 12 hp over the single-motor."

What it costs you

This deal is the single-motor GT (157 kW, 213 hp, FWD). You're on the efficient version. The dual motor costs more to lease and delivers ~3–4 mph higher 0–62 (to 8.7 sec vs. 9.7 sec) for a real-world efficiency drop. Irrelevant here.

Verdict: Not your problem. This deal sidesteps the complaint entirely.

Who This Deal Is For

✓ You have off-street charging (driveway or assigned space with power access)

Home charging at 7p/kWh night electricity transforms the e-5008 into a low-fuel-cost family car. The efficiency criticism evaporates. Annual energy cost: ~£106 on 5,000 miles.

✓ Annual mileage is genuinely under 6,000–7,000 miles

The 5,000-mile allowance is tight. Motorway commutes (London to Birmingham twice weekly, for example) will blow through it and incur ~11p/mile excess charges (~£550 for 5,000 overmiles). If you're local-only, this is irrelevant.

✓ You value boot space and seven seats over efficiency

The e-5008 is spacious and comfortable. It's not a sports car, but it's a practical family hauler. You're buying flexibility, not economy.

✗ You don't have home charging or rely on public rapid charging

This deal doesn't work for you. Without driveway charging, the e-5008's real-world 3.3 mi/kWh efficiency means rapid public charging at 30p/kWh becomes expensive (~£73 per 200 miles). The lease payment is still £259, but your total monthly cost rises to £450+. A more efficient EV (BYD Atto 3 or Kia EV6, if eligible for cheaper lease rates) would cost you less overall.

✗ You motorway-commute regularly

If you drive London–Bristol routes weekly, the e-5008's 170–200 mile real-world motorway range will force two rapid charges per leg. At 30p/kWh, that's £40 per 200 miles. A Polestar 3 or Tesla Model Y (if lease rates are competitive in your region) would stretch further between charges. Check if excess mileage fees (11p/mile over 5,000 annually) make this lease economically painful.

Three Checks Before You Sign

Check 1: Excess Mileage Cost

This deal includes 5,000 miles/year for 24 months = 10,000 miles total. The e-5008 is a family car; if you're averaging 6,500 miles/year, you'll pay ~£550 excess (11p/mile for 3,000 overmiles). Budget this into your decision.

Check 2: Home Charging Timeline

If you're considering this lease but haven't yet installed driveway charging, confirm: (a) your property allows it (landlord, leasehold permission?), and (b) installers can complete it before delivery (October 2026). Public rapid charging makes this deal uncompetitive. A 7 kW home charger (typical £400–1,200 installed) is table-stakes.

Check 3: Battery Warranty & Degradation

Peugeot's battery warranty covers 8 years or 160,000 km (~100,000 miles, whichever comes first) with ≥70% capacity retention. On a 24-month lease, you won't see degradation. Confirm the warranty is transferred to the leaseholder and not voided by non-dealership servicing (leased vehicles must use the Peugeot dealer network for warranty compliance).
