The Criticisms, Priced Out

1. Range: 285–321 Miles WLTP

The concern

EV Database lists two WLTP figures for this model, 285 miles and 321 miles, depending on the test energy setting. Real-world range will be lower in cold weather and on motorways.

What it costs you

At home, nothing: you charge overnight and start each day with a full battery. On public rapid charging at 30p/kWh, a 10,000-mile year costs about £1,000 in electricity, which is still roughly £705 less than the petrol comparison. The cost is in time, not money.

2. Charging Speed: 130 kW Peak

The concern

The 65.2 kWh usable battery takes up to 130 kW DC. That is respectable but not class-leading for a car at this price.

What it costs you

Only on long trips relying on fast chargers. For a daily driver with home charging, the peak rate is irrelevant.

3. The Grant Question

The concern

The Electric Car Grant gives £3,750 off eligible cars. Its published price cap is under £37,000 recommended retail price, rising to £42,000 with optional extras. This car's list price is £40,505, so it may not qualify at all, and the discount itself may not be sufficient to change that.

What it costs you

If the grant applies to this quote, the price falls by £3,750 on top of the discount. If it does not, the £30,365 is the full price. The dealer has not said which applies. Ask in writing before you commit.

Who This Deal Is For

✓ You have off-street charging

At 7p/kWh, the running cost is a fraction of petrol. The efficiency question stops mattering.

✓ You want an estate body and a premium badge at a lower price

The base eDrive20 is listed by EV Database at £37,065 on the road, or £33,315 after the £3,750 grant. This Tech+ quote at £30,365 is below both figures, but the trim and options differ, so compare like with like before deciding.

✗ You rely on public rapid charging

The saving over petrol shrinks but does not disappear. Even at 30p/kWh the car is cheaper to run than a 40 mpg petrol car. It is a worse deal than home charging, not a bad one.

✗ You cannot confirm the grant position

If the dealer will not confirm in writing whether the £3,750 applies, walk away until they do. The gap between the two outcomes is large.

Three Checks Before You Sign

Check 1: Get the grant position in writing

Ask the dealer whether the Electric Car Grant is applied to this quote, and which price it is measured against. Do not rely on the listing.

Check 2: Compare the full cost, not just the discount

A cash purchase carries depreciation, insurance and finance costs that this article does not estimate. Ask for insurance quotes and a resale estimate before comparing against a lease or a PCP deal.

Check 3: Confirm the trim and options

The listing shows no options selected, so £30,365 is the base trim price. Each option you add, such as the £1,100 driving assistant professional pack or the £1,100 panoramic sunroof, raises the price. Confirm the final figure with the dealer.
