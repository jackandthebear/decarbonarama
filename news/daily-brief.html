☀️ Solar

⚡ Perovskite-silicon tandem solar cell hits record 34% efficiency

Researchers at Soochow University and LONGi's Central R&D Institute engineered an ultra-thin zirconia nanoparticle interface that lets perovskite film grow smoothly while suppressing charge losses, pushing a tandem cell to 34.0% lab efficiency (33.5% certified) and a record 2.014V open-circuit voltage. The cell retained 84% of its output after 2,000 hours of continuous simulated sunlight, tackling tandem solar's durability problem alongside its efficiency gains.

Source: Now.Solar / Interesting Engineering

Smoother perovskite films lift triple-junction cell to 30.1% efficiency

A separate research team set a new efficiency record of 30.1% for three-layer perovskite solar cells by improving the smoothness of the perovskite films in the stack. Better film quality cuts down on defects that trap charge carriers, and is one of the main levers researchers are pulling to close the gap between lab results and commercial perovskite performance.

Source: Now.Solar / Tech Xplore

🔋 Battery Technology

⚡ US grid battery deployments hit record 18.9 GWh in Q2

The US installed 5.4 GW / 18.9 GWh of battery storage in Q2 2026, per the American Clean Power Association and Wood Mackenzie — a 17% year-over-year jump in energy capacity even as power capacity dipped slightly. Average storage duration rose from 2.8 to 3.5 hours and system prices fell 2% to $916/kW, with cumulative capacity on track to reach roughly 207 GW/715 GWh by 2031, driven partly by data-center demand.

Source: Solar Quarter

Mercedes-Benz locks in early access to ProLogium's solid-state EV cells

Mercedes-Benz secured priority access to ProLogium's Gen4 solid-state battery cells, which have shown up to 400 Wh/kg energy density and 5%-80% charging in about 6.4 minutes in third-party tests. The automaker will run its own safety and performance testing, with a final agreement expected by year-end and Mercedes' first solid-state-powered EV targeted for 2030 — a sign solid-state is edging closer to production cars rather than staying a lab demo.

Source: Electrek

💨 Wind

Germany's largest Baltic Sea offshore wind farm reaches financial close

The 976.5 MW Gennaker offshore wind farm off Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania reached financial close after developer Skyborn Renewables secured over €3 billion from 16 lenders, the European Investment Bank and Stadtwerke München. Its 63 Siemens Gamesa turbines are expected to generate about 3.8 TWh a year — enough for roughly a million households — with installation starting in 2027 and operation targeted for 2028.

Source: Offshore Wind

⚛️ Nuclear

US and Turkey sign SMR grant deal to diversify nuclear supply chain

The US Trade and Development Agency and Turkey's state nuclear developer TUNAS signed a roughly $2 million grant funding at least a year of technical work on regulatory, institutional and financial frameworks for small modular reactors in Turkey. It's a modest sum but a strategic signal: Ankara wants US technology to counterbalance Russia's Rosatom, which is building its $20 billion Akkuyu plant, as Turkey targets 12 large reactors plus 5,000 MW of SMR capacity by 2050.

Source: Al-Monitor

⚡ NuScale in talks for up to 6 GW of SMRs with the Tennessee Valley Authority

NuScale Power is negotiating a framework deal with TVA in which partner ENTRA1 Energy would build the plants and NuScale would supply the reactors — up to 6 GW of capacity, enough for about 4.5 million homes or roughly 60 data centers, and a 6.2% boost to total US nuclear output. The agreement is still non-binding with no power purchase deal signed, so the payoff for cash-strapped NuScale (Q2 revenue was just $75,000) remains uncertain.

Source: Nuclear News / The Motley Fool

Private fusion investment keeps flowing as records fall

An industry roundup highlighted continued momentum in private fusion: Helion Energy's Polaris prototype reached 150 million°C using deuterium-tritium fuel — the only private machine to do so — as it works toward a 50+ MW Orion plant by 2028, while newcomer Inertia Enterprises raised a $450 million Series A led by Bessemer Venture Partners and Google Ventures for its "Thunderwall" laser fusion technology.

Source: ANS Nuclear Newswire

♨️ Heat Pumps

France tightens rules on its heat-pump subsidy

As of September 1, France's "coup de pouce" heat-pump incentive now requires state-approved models carrying European certification and manufactured within the European Economic Area. Homes adding solar alongside a heat pump must now achieve full decarbonization of both space heating and hot water, with no fossil-fuel-capable equipment left in place — tightening the bar on one of Europe's most active heat-pump subsidy programs.

Source: Service-Public.fr

New England presses ahead on its $450 million heat-pump push

Connecticut, Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire and Rhode Island are rolling out a $450 million federally-funded initiative targeting 580,000 residential heat pump installs by 2030 — equivalent to taking more than half a million gas-powered cars off the road. The effort has taken on added weight since federal heat-pump tax credits expired at the end of 2025, leaving state programs to carry the electrification momentum.

Source: Harvest Thermal

💧 Hydro

⚡ Spain awards €265 million for 49.9 GWh of pumped hydro storage

Spain's Boralmac program handed out €265 million across two funding rounds to 11 pumped-hydro projects totalling 4.1 GW of turbine capacity and 49.9 GWh of storage across seven regions, due for completion by 2035. Standouts include the 545 MW Baells facility in Barcelona and the 354 MW La Barca project in Asturias; several projects reuse existing reservoirs or old mine sites to speed deployment and cut costs.

Source: pv magazine

93% of US hydropower capacity faces relicensing within a decade

A National Hydropower Association analysis finds 12,324 MW across 348 FERC licenses — roughly 93% of the US hydro fleet — comes up for relicensing between 2026 and 2036, just as grid load growth hits its steepest pace in decades. The industry's core complaint is that mandatory licensing conditions have multiplied, sometimes tenfold, while only a small share carry documented cost estimates, making it hard for owners to decide whether to relicense or retire hydro capacity the grid needs for reliability.

Source: mGrid

🌊 Wave & Tidal

⚡ PacWave South, the first grid-connected US wave energy test site, opens off Oregon

Oregon State University's PacWave South facility opened about seven miles off Newport, Oregon, with nearly 50 miles of seafloor cable and four grid-connected berths rated at 5 MW each — the first pre-permitted, grid-connected wave energy test site in the US. No wave-energy devices have been installed yet, with developers citing federal permitting delays, but the infrastructure itself is seen as a major unlock for US marine energy testing.

Source: CleanTechnica

Eco Wave Power nears 2026 launch of its Portuguese wave project

Eco Wave Power reported reaching a key construction milestone on its onshore-mounted wave energy project in Portugal, moving the company closer to a planned 2026 launch and its stated ambitions for global expansion of the technology.

Source: Eco Wave Power

Bottom line

Solar keeps setting efficiency records while grid batteries just posted their biggest quarter yet and solid-state cells edge toward real EVs — the hardware side of the transition keeps compounding. Big financings (a €3bn German offshore wind farm, €265m for Spanish pumped hydro) show utility-scale build-out continuing even as US hydro faces a wall of relicensing decisions. Nuclear's SMR pipeline is advancing through international and hyperscaler-driven deals, and private fusion funding hit new highs, though none of it is generating power yet. Heat pumps are being pushed forward by state and EU-level policy just as US federal incentives fade. And after years of pilot projects, wave energy notched a genuine first with a grid-connected US test site — though hardware to plug into it is still on the way.
