☀️ Solar

⚡ A gigawatt of solar signed in a single week, across four continents

Between 20 and 21 August, developers closed deals covering well over a gigawatt of new solar and hundreds of millions in fresh financing — Sol Systems buying the 200 MWac Lumberton project in Texas from DESRI, EDF Power Solutions signing two 25-year PPAs with NV Energy for a 400 MWac Nevada solar plant paired with a 400 MW / 1,600 MWh battery, ATOME advancing a 300 MWp project in Paraguay, and AMPIN Energy Transition raising roughly $195m for a hybrid renewable in India. The pattern to note is that almost none of these are solar alone — storage is now the default companion.

Source: Saur Energy

US on track for a record 43.4 GW of utility solar this year

The EIA expects the United States to add 43.4 GW of new utility-scale solar PV in 2026, which would be the largest single year on record. It is a useful counterweight to the noise about federal incentive rollbacks: the build pipeline set in motion over the past three years is still landing.

Source: PV Tech

⚡ Perovskite tandems now certified above 34%

LONGi holds an NREL-certified tandem record of 34.85%, comfortably past the 33.7% theoretical ceiling for single-junction silicon, and triple-junction perovskite-silicon devices set their own record earlier this year. Commercial modules remain a niche — Oxford PV's first shipments were 24.5% — but the lab-to-fab gap is closing, with mainstream availability generally pencilled in for 2027–28.

Source: TechXplore

🔋 Battery Technology

⚡ Sodium-ion crosses from prototype to product line

CATL has extended its sodium-ion range beyond vehicles into a platform battery built specifically for stationary storage, and says sodium cells will appear in passenger EVs by the end of this year. Sodium's appeal is structural rather than performance-led: cheaper raw materials, no lithium exposure, and markedly better cold-weather behaviour — all of which matter more for grid assets than for cars.

Source: ESS News

BYD and Greenvolt to build a 2.4 GWh storage plant in Poland

BYD Energy Storage and Greenvolt Power will co-develop a 600 MW / 2.4 GWh facility at Siedlce in eastern Poland, with construction due to start this quarter and operation targeted for end-2027. Poland's coal-heavy grid makes it one of the more consequential places in Europe to site storage at this scale.

Source: Battery-Tech Network

A cheap additive brings solid-state sodium closer to the grid

A National University of Singapore team led by Palani Balaya reports that a single low-cost additive resolves several of the interface problems that have held back all-solid-state sodium cells, pointing to a scalable route for grid storage that is both non-flammable and lithium-free. Still lab-stage, but it targets exactly the cost and safety constraints that decide whether long-duration storage gets built.

Source: TechXplore

India's first utility-scale non-lithium storage goes to flow batteries

Delectrik Systems won a tender to deploy a 100 MWh vanadium redox flow battery at the Khavda Solar Park — India's first utility-scale storage system that isn't lithium-ion. Flow and compressed-air are increasingly where the market turns for durations beyond the four-to-six hours lithium serves economically.

Source: iGrow News

💨 Wind

Revolution Wind hits 95% complete, with four turbines to go

Sixty-one of 65 turbines are now installed at the Rhode Island and Connecticut project, which remains on track for commissioning in the second half of this year. Given the project spent part of last year under a federal stop-work order, finishing on schedule is a meaningful signal for the rest of the US offshore pipeline.

Source: offshoreWIND.biz

All 111 monopiles installed at Baltica 2

The 1.5 GW Polish project reported on 21 August that its full foundation set — 107 turbine monopiles plus four for offshore substations — is in the seabed. Baltica 2 is the anchor project for Poland's offshore ambitions and one of the largest under construction in the Baltic.

Source: reNEWS

Nordex books 480 MW of US orders in a month

Nordex secured three US orders in August covering 81 N163/5.X turbines totalling more than 480 MW, all to be built through its US manufacturing network, plus a 77 MW order from OX2 in Romania. Domestic production is doing real work here — it insulates projects from tariff exposure and from a rare-earth supply chain still dominated by China.

Source: GreentechLead

⚛️ Nuclear

⚡ Commonwealth Fusion raises another $1bn

CFS has closed a further $1 billion in equity — the largest fusion round anywhere since its own $1.8bn Series B in 2021 — as it works toward net-energy demonstration at SPARC. Private fusion capital has not cooled, and the round sets a new benchmark for what the sector can raise pre-revenue.

Source: World Nuclear News

ITER installs its sixth sector module, six months ahead of schedule

The sixth of nine tokamak sector modules is now in the pit at Cadarache, running roughly half a year early. After a decade of slipped milestones, a project of ITER's size beating its own timeline is worth noting on its own terms.

Source: ANS Nuclear Newswire

Rolls-Royce SMRs picked for a 1.4 GW Swedish plant

Videberg Kraft is developing a 1.4 GW station on Sweden's west coast built around three Rolls-Royce small modular reactors, while fellow Swedish developer Blykalla filed a second SMR plant application this month. Sweden is quietly becoming the busiest SMR market in Europe.

Source: ANS Nuclear Newswire

South Korea names SMRs and fusion national growth industries

Seoul has designated small modular reactors and nuclear fusion among seven next-generation industries earmarked for state backing. For a country with an established reactor export business, the move signals an attempt to carry that position into the SMR era rather than cede it.

Source: UPI

♨️ Heat Pumps

⚡ New England's five states pool $450m into a heat pump accelerator

Connecticut, Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire and Rhode Island have launched a coordinated $450 million New England Heat Pump Accelerator to speed residential uptake. The timing is deliberate: the federal 25C tax credit for air-source heat pumps expired at the end of 2025, and states are moving to fill the gap rather than let installation rates stall.

Source: Harvest Thermal

Oregon now requires heat pumps in new homes

From this year, new residential buildings in Oregon must install a heat pump rather than conventional air conditioning — a quiet but effective piece of policy design, since it targets the cooling equipment homes were going to buy anyway. California, separately, is pushing its Heat Pump Access Act to standardise permitting statewide.

Source: HVAC365

Cold-climate performance is no longer the objection

Field data on units installed between 2023 and 2025 shows reliable operation down to around −25°C, and installers in several developed markets now report heat pumps outselling gas boiler and furnace replacements. The barrier has shifted decisively from "does it work in winter" to upfront cost and electricity-to-gas price ratios.

Source: What is Smart Energy?

💧 Hydro

⚡ Global pumped storage passes 200 GW

The International Hydropower Association's 2026 World Hydropower Outlook records 28 GW of hydropower commissioned worldwide last year, including a record 11.6 GW of pumped storage — pushing global "water battery" capacity beyond 200 GW for the first time. It remains, by a wide margin, the largest form of grid storage on the planet.

Source: International Hydropower Association

US industry pitches pumped storage as the answer to data-centre demand

A National Hydropower Association report argues the US cannot meet AI and advanced-manufacturing load growth without new pumped storage, noting Google and Microsoft both signed long-term hydropower supply agreements in North America last year. More than 60 GW of pumped storage is now in the US development pipeline, though permitting timelines remain the binding constraint.

Source: ASCE Civil Engineering Source

Business rates row clouds Scotland's Earba scheme

Critics argue Scottish business rates are deterring investment in large storage projects including the Earba pumped hydro scheme in the Highlands, one of the biggest proposed in Britain. It is a reminder that for long-lived assets, tax treatment over decades can matter as much as the capital grant at the start.

Source: No2NuclearPower

🌊 Wave & Tidal

Oregon's wave test site opens — with nothing yet to test

PacWave, the first large grid-connected wave energy test site in the continental US, has opened off the Oregon coast after clearing its final approvals. No devices have arrived yet: developers are held up by delays in the federal funding that was meant to pay for them, so the berths sit empty for now.

Source: OPB

⚡ CorPower's C4 takes the first DNV certificate for a wave converter

CorPower Ocean's C4 device, running off Aguçadoura in Portugal since 2023 and already survivor of 18.5 m waves, received the first DNV Prototype Certificate ever issued to a wave energy converter. Certification is the unglamorous step that unlocks insurance and project finance, and CorPower is now progressing multi-megawatt arrays in Portugal and at EMEC in Orkney.

Source: Developments in Tidal and Wave Electricity

UK tidal stream past 100 MW under CfD; France preparing a 250 MW tender

Britain's Contracts for Difference rounds have now underwritten more than 100 MW of tidal stream capacity for delivery in the late 2020s, and France is preparing a 250 MW tidal tender under its multi-year energy programme. Tidal remains expensive per MWh, but its output is perfectly predictable years in advance — a property no other renewable offers.

Source: Yale E360

Bottom line

Two threads run through this morning's news. The first is that storage has stopped being a separate category: the Nevada solar PPA, the Australian wind hub, BYD's Polish plant and the 200 GW pumped storage milestone are all the same story about firming variable generation, and sodium-ion is emerging as the cost lever that makes stationary storage cheap rather than merely possible. The second is the growing gap between where policy support is expiring and where it is arriving — the US federal heat pump credit has lapsed just as five New England states put $450m on the table, and PacWave has berths but no devices because federal marine funding is stuck. Fusion, meanwhile, keeps clearing bars it has never cleared before: another $1bn for Commonwealth, and ITER running six months early for once. None of it changes this year's emissions, but the 2030s pipeline looks steadily more real.
