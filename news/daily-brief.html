🔋 Battery Technology

⚡ CATL says sodium-ion will reach cost parity with LFP this year

CATL expects sodium-ion cells to match lithium iron phosphate on cost by the end of 2026, with further declines as lines scale. Its cells retain over 90% capacity at −20°C and deliver roughly three times LFP's discharge power at −30°C — a strong fit for cold-climate grid storage where lithium supply security is a worry.

Source: SodiumBatteryHub

Bottom line

The theme this morning is cost and certification rather than lab records. CATL calling sodium-ion parity with LFP by year-end would remove lithium supply risk from the grid-storage equation entirely, while pumped hydro quietly passing 200 GW is a reminder of how much storage already exists in concrete and water. Manufacturing keeps landing in the US on security grounds — LG in Michigan, Forge Nano in North Carolina with DOE money — and datacentre demand is now the argument being made for everything from pumped storage to SMRs. In the UK, the £9,000 off-grid boiler upgrade grant and the 2027 new-build gas ban are the two levers that matter for domestic heat, and 180,000 installations against a 600,000-a-year target tells you how much ground is left. And after two decades of promises, a DNV certificate for a wave device is the most consequential marine energy news in years, because it is what turns a prototype into something a bank will lend against.
