☀️ Solar

⚡ US on track for a record 43.4 GW of utility-scale solar in 2026

EIA figures put this year's large-scale solar additions 60% above 2025 and more than 12 GW clear of the previous record, making solar 51% of all new US generating capacity in 2026. Texas alone accounts for roughly 40% of the build (17.4 GW), with Arizona and California each near 6%.

Source: PV Tech

Perovskite tandems cross from record chart to production line

LONGi's NREL-certified 34.85% perovskite-silicon tandem cell remains the benchmark, and 2026 is the first year tandem modules are being produced commercially rather than just demonstrated. Residential availability is still thin — most analysts point to 2027–28 before homeowners see tandem panels on quotes.

Source: Patsnap

Chinese team claims world record for inverted perovskite cell

Researchers at Nankai University and the Beijing Institute of Technology report 27.17% power conversion efficiency for an inverted-architecture perovskite cell. Inverted (p-i-n) designs are the structure most tandem manufacturers are betting on for stability, so records here matter more commercially than raw lab numbers suggest.

Source: pv magazine

🔋 Battery Technology

Second-quarter US clean energy and transport investment hit $75 billion, up 4% year-on-year and the second-highest quarter on record. The rebound was driven by consumers: EV, home battery and heat pump purchases rose 21% to $41 billion, offsetting softness in industrial project spend.

Source: Forbes

⚡ Forge Nano and Samsung SDI win $100m for US lithium-ion hub

The pair announced a partnership backed by a $100 million grant from the federal Battery Materials Processing and Battery Manufacturing programme, aimed at a domestic advanced lithium-ion manufacturing base. Heavy-duty trucking, aerospace and defence are the stated end markets rather than passenger EVs.

Source: CleanTechnica

Tesla Shanghai passes six million battery packs as Megafactory ramps

Tesla's Shanghai plant rolled off its six millionth pack this week while construction continues on the adjacent Megafactory for Megapack grid storage. It is a useful marker of how quickly stationary storage manufacturing is being bolted onto existing EV supply chains.

Source: EnergyTrend

Sodium-ion moves from curiosity to contracted supply

CATL and Beijing HyperStrong's three-year, 60 GWh sodium-ion storage agreement — the largest yet announced — is now feeding a deployment wave, with CATL's Naxtra cells rated from −40°C to 70°C. Cheaper feedstock and cold tolerance make sodium a credible stationary-storage alternative to LFP rather than a lab novelty.

Source: CleanTechnica

The C4 machine operating off Aguçadoura in Portugal since 2023 has become the first wave energy converter to receive a DNV Prototype Certificate. Certification is the gate institutional lenders have been waiting for; CorPower is now progressing multi-megawatt arrays in Portugal and at EMEC in Orkney.

Orbital Marine, SIMEC Atlantis and Nova Innovation are all advancing multi-MW arrays, giving the sector its most credible commercial pipeline in two decades of trying. The open question remains cost per MWh at array scale rather than whether the machines work.

Source: Tethys / PNNL

Bottom line

The money story this morning is a two-speed one: US consumer-facing clean tech — EVs, home batteries, heat pumps — pulled Q2 investment back up to $75bn, while the federal government spent another $1.22bn paying developers not to build offshore wind. Europe is quietly doing the opposite, consenting 4.5 GW of offshore projects in a week and energising three more farms. Storage is the sector with the most genuine momentum: sodium-ion has moved to 60 GWh supply contracts, and pumped hydro has crossed 200 GW globally on a record year, increasingly underwritten by data-centre demand. Fusion had its best financing day in five years with Commonwealth's second billion-dollar raise, and ITER is — remarkably — running ahead of schedule. For UK homeowners, the practical news is the £9,000 off-gas-grid heat pump grant, which is where the retrofit economics now actually stack up.
