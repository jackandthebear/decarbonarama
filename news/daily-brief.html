☀️ Solar

⚡ Nanjing team certifies 22% efficiency on metre-scale perovskite modules

Research led by Xiao Ke and Hairen Tan at Nanjing University, published this month in Nature, reports a certified 22.0% full-area efficiency on metre-scale perovskite hardware. Scaling perovskite from postage-stamp lab cells to real module sizes has been the technology's biggest bottleneck, so a certified result at this area is a meaningful step toward manufacturable panels.

Source: Interesting Engineering

Tesla signs two of the largest solar PPAs on record in the US Southwest

Tesla has agreed a major power purchase agreement with UK-based ContourGlobal and a second deal with Zelestra, adding to a run of very large corporate offtake contracts. The company has also filed plans for a $10.1bn vertically integrated solar manufacturing campus in Fort Bend County, Texas.

Source: Solar Builder

EIA: US on track for a record 43.4 GW of utility-scale solar this year

The Energy Information Administration's August Short-Term Energy Outlook forecasts 43.4 GW of new utility-scale PV in 2026 — a 60% jump on 2025 and more than 12 GW above 2024. Solar generation was already up 21% year on year in the first half of 2026.

Source: PV Tech

Scatec completes gigawatt-scale Obelisk solar-plus-storage in Egypt

Scatec has begun commercial operations at the second and final phase of its Obelisk project, one of Africa's largest solar-plus-storage builds. Pairing large PV with batteries is increasingly the default configuration in high-irradiance markets where evening demand is the binding constraint.

Source: PV Tech

🔋 Battery Technology

⚡ DOE offers Eos Energy a $398.6m loan guarantee for zinc battery lines

The Department of Energy's Loan Programs Office issued a conditional commitment of up to $398.6 million to Eos Energy Enterprises to build up to four production lines for its Z3 zinc-based storage systems. It is one of the larger federal backstops for a non-lithium stationary storage chemistry, aimed at domesticating long-duration supply.

Source: US Department of Energy

⚡ CATL confirms solid-state trial production for 2027 at 500 Wh/kg

CATL said on 10 August that its all-solid-state battery will enter small-scale trial production in 2027, with early sulfide cells hitting roughly 500 Wh/kg in the lab — about double today's best mass-produced lithium-ion. Trial production is still a long way from volume, but it puts a date on a technology that has slipped repeatedly.

Source: Intelligent Living

Sodium-ion moves from curiosity to grid-scale reality

Sodium-ion has topped MIT Technology Review's breakthrough technologies list for 2026, and CATL's Naxtra line — rated from −40°C to 70°C — is now in mass production for stationary use. Sodium still accounts for only about 4% of existing or funded global cell capacity, so the near-term impact is on cold-climate and cost-sensitive niches rather than the whole market.

Source: Yale E360

Tallahassee approves $39m battery system for underserved neighbourhoods

Tallahassee's municipal utility is building a utility-scale battery at the Birmingham Street substation, funded by a $28.7m DOE grant plus a $10.7m city match. It is a small project by megawatt count but a notable example of storage being sited explicitly for local reliability rather than pure arbitrage.

Source: Government Market News

Britain's seventh CfD allocation round awarded 14.7 GW across 201 projects — the largest in the scheme's history — including a record 8.4 GW of offshore wind and 1.3 GW of onshore wind at £72.24/MWh. Cornwall's Imerys wind farm was the biggest English onshore project to win a contract in a decade.

The IHA's 2026 World Hydropower Outlook confirms global pumped storage capacity has crossed 201 GW after a record 11.6 GW of additions in 2025. The "water battery" still provides the overwhelming majority of the world's grid-scale storage energy, despite the attention batteries attract.

🌊 Wave & Tidal

The Morlais tidal-stream site off Holy Island, backed by €37.6m of EU regional development grant funding, is consented for up to 240 MW and has its first turbine deployments scheduled for this year. It remains the largest consented tidal-stream zone in Europe and the key proving ground for array-scale tidal.

Source: International Water Power

Ocean energy still measured in the low hundreds of megawatts

By mid-2026 total installed ocean energy capacity worldwide remains in the low-to-mid hundreds of MW, most of it from two large tidal-range barrages; tidal-stream arrays and wave converters are still at demonstration or early pre-commercial scale. Forecasters nevertheless project the sector growing from about $11.3bn in 2025 to $116bn by 2036.

Source: Developments of Tidal and Wave Electricity

Bottom line

The split between US and European policy is now the defining story of the transition. Washington has spent nearly $4bn buying developers out of offshore wind leases — RWE taking $1.2bn and redirecting it into LNG — while Britain's AR7 auction contracted a record 14.7 GW and Sweden lines up Rolls-Royce SMRs. Yet US solar is booming regardless, with the EIA forecasting a record 43.4 GW this year, which says something about how far economics have decoupled from policy support in mature technologies. On the tech side, the week's substance is in storage and perovskites: a certified 22% metre-scale perovskite module, CATL putting a 2027 date on solid-state trial production, and a $398.6m federal loan guarantee for zinc batteries. And a quiet reminder from the IHA that pumped hydro just passed 200 GW — still, by a wide margin, the world's real energy storage fleet.
