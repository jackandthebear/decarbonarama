☀️ Solar

⚡ Texas coal mine to host North America's largest coal-site solar build

Panamint Capital has broken ground on the Big Rooter Power project at its Twin Oaks power station in Robertson County, eastern Texas — a roughly $1.7bn investment and the largest solar project built on a former coal mining site in North America. Phase one (491 MWdc) is targeted for August 2028, with a second 658 MWdc phase following in 2029. Reusing mine land sidesteps the interconnection and permitting delays that slow greenfield sites.

Source: pv magazine USA

US on track for a record 43.4 GW of utility-scale solar this year

EIA figures point to developers adding 43.4 GW of utility-scale solar PV in 2026, a roughly 60% jump on the record 27.2 GW installed in 2025. Texas alone accounts for around 40% of new projects. Solar and storage together are expected to lead an 86 GW capacity surge across the US grid this year.

Source: PV Tech

Perovskite tandems hold at 34.85% as commercial modules trickle out

LONGi's NREL-certified 34.85% perovskite-silicon tandem cell remains the benchmark, comfortably past the 33.7% single-junction theoretical ceiling, and Trina set a fresh module-level record in June. Commercial volumes are still thin — Oxford PV's tandem panels ship at 24.5% module efficiency — with mainstream rooftop availability more realistically a 2027–28 story.

Source: CleanTechnica

India's ACME signs 300 MW wind-solar hybrid PPA at 2.9p/kWh

ACME Solar has secured a 25-year power purchase agreement with SECI for a 300 MW interstate-transmission-connected wind-solar hybrid at $0.034/kWh. Hybrid tariffs at that level make round-the-clock renewable supply competitive with new thermal generation in India.

Source: pv magazine

🔋 Battery Technology

⚡ CATL pushes 5 GWh of sodium-ion into grid storage

CATL is deploying 5 GWh of its Tener sodium-ion batteries, leaning on a claimed 15,000-cycle life to displace lithium in stationary storage. It follows April's 60 GWh sodium supply agreement with integrator HyperStrong — the largest sodium order ever placed — and a German launch for the Tener system in June. Grid storage doesn't need lithium's energy density; it needs cycle life, safety and cost, which is exactly where sodium now competes.

Source: Electrek

Sodium forecast to undercut LFP on cost by 2027

Analysts now expect sodium-ion cells — particularly NFPP chemistry — to fall below lithium iron phosphate on $/kWh within roughly 18 months, driven by cheap feedstock and rapidly scaling Chinese capacity. US manufacturers are following, with pilot grid-scale and solar-paired projects running in California and New York.

Source: Sodium Battery Hub

Stellantis and Factorial validate a 375 Wh/kg semi-solid-state cell

The validated cell beats the typical 200–300 Wh/kg of conventional lithium-ion and is claimed to charge 15–90% in 18 minutes at room temperature, with commercial production slated to begin this year. Separately, first A-sample all-solid-state cells have come off production lines having passed needle-penetration, extrusion and thermal-shock testing without fire.

Source: InsideEVs

💨 Wind

Thirty-six of 72 Siemens Gamesa turbines — each up to 15 MW — are now installed at Thor off the Danish west coast, with the installation vessel Brave Tern working out of Esbjerg. Installation completes by the end of 2026 and commissioning is targeted for 2027, making it Denmark's largest offshore wind farm.

Source: Offshore Magazine

Crown Estate relaunches tender for the Morgan offshore site

The Crown Estate has reopened bidding for the Morgan site off England after EnBW and JERA Nex bp withdrew, with a replacement developer expected to be named by the end of 2026. The re-tender is a reminder that UK offshore economics remain tight even on well-characterised seabed.

Source: Offshore Magazine

Nordex books 3.1 GW of onshore orders in Q2, up 32%

The Hamburg turbine maker's second-quarter intake rose from 2.3 GW a year earlier, with strong demand from its German home market including corporate buyers such as Continental. Order books at this level suggest European onshore wind is running well ahead of last year despite policy noise elsewhere.

Source: Renewables Now

Van Oord completes seabed protection at Hollandse Kust West VI

Some 300,000 tonnes of rock have been placed to protect turbine foundations and cable infrastructure at Ecowende's Dutch site, which has already exported first power. Full operation is expected by the end of 2026.

The agency will unveil Atomic Technologies Licensed for Applications at Sea at its ministerial in Washington DC on 26–27 August, aimed at supporting SMRs for civilian shipping and offshore power. A credible international licensing route is the main obstacle to marine nuclear, so the framework matters more than the hardware right now.

Oklo received clearance on 24 July to begin start-up of its test reactor, following Aalo Atomics' Critical Test Reactor at Idaho National Laboratory reaching initial criticality in early July — the fourth microreactor under US Department of Energy authorisation to do so. Hardware is finally moving from paper to criticality in the US microreactor field.

♨️ Heat Pumps

⚡ Warm Homes Plan puts £15bn behind home retrofit

January's Warm Homes Plan allocates £15bn across schemes including a topped-up Boiler Upgrade Scheme and £2bn of low-interest loans covering heat pumps, solar PV and batteries. The £7,500 BUS grant remains in place for air-to-water and ground source installations — still the single biggest lever on UK household economics.

Source: Grant UK

MCS 020(a) becomes the sole noise-compliance route in England

Since 28 May, MCS 020(a) is the only certification scheme accepted as proof of noise compliance for permitted-development heat pump installs in England. It follows a package of planning reforms: the 1-metre boundary rule removed, maximum unit size raised from 0.6m³ to 1.5m³, detached houses allowed two air source units, and combined heating-and-cooling units made eligible.

Source: Cucumber Eco

Installations growing, but the 600,000 target still looks distant

More than 125,000 heat pumps were sold in the UK in 2025, with certified installations up around 11% year on year — real momentum, but roughly 1% of UK homes are heated this way and the government target is 600,000 installations a year by 2028. Industry bodies are pressing for policy certainty on non-domestic buildings, where the incentive picture is thinnest.

Source: Heat Pump Association

Source: Business Standard

Up to three ageing turbines will be replaced with "wide-head" units designed to keep generating at much lower Lake Mead elevations, potentially restoring at least 160 MW of capacity. It sits within a broader DOE package of nearly $430m across 293 projects at 212 US hydropower facilities.

Source: International Water Power

Bottom line

Storage is where the centre of gravity has moved. Sodium-ion has gone from curiosity to 60 GWh purchase orders in a year and is now forecast to undercut LFP on cost by 2027, while pumped hydro has crossed 200 GW globally and India alone has 16 GW in build — though Queensland's cancellation is a reminder that big civil projects still die on economics. Solar's story is volume rather than novelty: 43.4 GW of new US utility-scale capacity this year, with perovskite tandems still stuck at record-cell level rather than shipped modules. Nuclear finally has hardware going critical, with Oklo and Aalo both hitting milestones in the past fortnight. For UK households the practical news remains the £15bn Warm Homes Plan and the loosened permitted-development rules — the friction on getting a heat pump fitted is lower than it has ever been, even if the 600,000-a-year target still looks a long way off.
