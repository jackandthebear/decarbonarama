Peak Energy's 183,000 sq ft Sacramento facility — a $71m investment — will produce up to 4 GWh of sodium-ion storage annually from early 2027. It is the first serious attempt to prove sodium-ion can deliver cheaper, safer grid storage at a scale that actually shifts the US electricity system away from lithium supply chains.

Advanced cells are closing the energy-density gap with entry-level lithium iron phosphate, which would open sodium-ion to applications well beyond stationary storage. Sodium is around a thousand times more abundant than lithium and extractable from seawater — the appeal is supply security as much as cost.

Bottom line

The theme this morning is storage in every form. Sodium-ion is moving from pilot to factory in the US, pumped hydro has passed 200 GW globally with Dinorwig getting another quarter-century, and both are answers to the same problem: what to do with a grid that now adds 43 GW of solar a year. On the demand side, the UK's £9,000 off-gas-grid heat pump grant and New England's $450m accelerator show state and national governments picking up where the expired US federal credit left off — and US heat pump sales outselling gas furnaces without that credit suggests the economics may no longer need it. Fusion keeps attracting extraordinary capital, with CFS raising another $1bn and ITER, remarkably, running ahead of schedule. Offshore wind remains the awkward outlier: Europe is delivering, but Coastal Virginia's $300m cost rise and slipped timeline show the US supply chain still hasn't found its footing.
