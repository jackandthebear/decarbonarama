Texas coal plant site to be shared with a large solar-plus-storage build

The Twin Oaks coal plant at Bremond, Texas will host a co-located solar-and-battery project on the same site, reusing an existing grid interconnection rather than queuing for a new one. Repowering retiring fossil sites is becoming one of the fastest routes to new capacity in a market where interconnection queues run to years.

⚡ EIA expects a record 43.4 GW of US utility-scale solar in 2026

Developers are on track to add more utility-scale solar this year than in any previous year, with Texas taking roughly 40% of the total — including Solar Proponent's 837 MW Tehuacana Creek 1, the largest single project due online in 2026. The forecast holds despite a sharp first-quarter slowdown, implying a heavily back-loaded year.

Source: PV Tech

⚡ Perovskite tandems keep setting records as commercial modules ship

Trinasolar has claimed a world record for a silicon module with a perovskite top cell, following LONGi's 34.85% NREL-certified tandem cell and a 27.17% inverted single-junction record from Nankai University. Oxford PV's commercial tandem panels are now quoted around 29.7% module efficiency — the gap between lab and product is closing faster than most 2023 roadmaps assumed.

SEIA's Q2 market report puts first-quarter utility-scale additions at 5.9 GWdc, down 34% on a year earlier and 45% quarter-on-quarter. Some of this is normal winter seasonality, but it sets up a demanding rest-of-year run rate if the record annual forecast is to land.

🔋 Battery Technology

⚡ CATL pushes sodium-ion into mainstream grid storage

CATL is deploying 5 GWh of its Tener sodium-ion product and has signed a 60 GWh supply agreement with integrator HyperStrong — the largest sodium order placed to date. The pitch is a 15,000-cycle life and wide-temperature tolerance: grid storage does not need lithium's energy density, it needs cycles, safety and low cost.

Source: Electrek

GM backs a US sodium-ion platform for grid storage

General Motors has put weight behind Peak Energy, which shipped the first US grid-scale sodium-ion system and is promising 20-year durability and safer high-temperature operation. It is a notable signal that sodium is being taken seriously in a market where domestic supply chain risk now prices as heavily as cell cost.

Source: IEEE Spectrum

Envision and Kallista break ground on a 386 MWh battery in France

The 193 MW / 386 MWh Neuilly-en-Thelle project started construction in early July and is due to begin frequency-regulation services for the French grid in 2028. It is the pair's second collaboration, pointing to a repeatable pipeline rather than a one-off.

Source: ESS News

Chile advances a 200 MW / 1,000 MWh storage project

A Chilean developer has updated plans for a five-hour, gigawatt-hour-scale battery — the sort of long-duration configuration that suits a grid with very high midday solar output and a steep evening ramp. Chile has become one of the most storage-dense markets outside China and the US.

Source: ESS News

⚡ Google–Xcel deal includes what is billed as the world's largest grid battery

The agreement pairs 2.5 GW of generation with 2.9 GWh of storage for delivery by 2029, tied to data-centre load growth in Xcel's territory. Hyperscaler procurement is now large enough to reshape individual utilities' resource plans.

Source: Utility Dive

⚛️ Nuclear

⚡ Aalo Atomics microreactor reaches initial criticality at Idaho

Aalo's Critical Test Reactor at Idaho National Laboratory achieved a self-sustaining chain reaction, becoming the fourth microreactor under DOE authorisation to do so by the programme's 4 July 2026 deadline. The DOE Reactor Pilot Program had targeted three; it got four, which is a rare case of a nuclear schedule being beaten rather than slipped.

Source: World Nuclear News

Oklo cleared to start up its test reactor

Oklo received clearance on 24 July to begin startup of its test reactor, another step along the same DOE pilot pathway. The cluster of approvals suggests the licensing bottleneck for very small reactors is genuinely loosening, at least for test units on federal sites.

Source: ANS Nuclear Newswire

⚡ ITER cools its first magnet coil to 4 Kelvin

The ITER Organization has started operations at its Magnet Cold Test Facility after successfully cooling the first coil to 4 K. Testing the magnets at operating temperature is one of the highest-risk items on ITER's critical path, so a clean first cooldown matters more than its low profile suggests.

Source: World Nuclear News

DOE approves Xcimer's Athena fusion plant roadmap

The Department of Energy formally signed off Xcimer Energy's preconceptual design and technology development roadmap for Athena, its laser-driven fusion power plant architecture. It is a milestone gate rather than a physics result, but it keeps inertial fusion funded on the back of the NIF ignition work.

Source: Fusion Industry Association

♨️ Heat Pumps

⚡ UK raises the Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant to £9,000 for oil and LPG homes

From 21 July 2026 rural households replacing oil heating with a heat pump can claim £9,000 rather than £7,500 — the largest single uplift the scheme has made, running to March 2027. It targets the 1.7 million off-gas-grid homes where heat pump economics are already strongest, since oil is expensive and unregulated.

Source: pv magazine

Record UK installs in 2025, but growth is slowing

UK heat pump installations hit a record 125,037 in 2025, yet growth decelerated to 27% — below the roughly 33% compound rate needed to reach the government's 450,000-a-year target by 2030. Record volumes and a missed trajectory are both true at once, which is why the policy noise has increased.

Source: HeatPumpsAndSolar

Clean Heat Market Mechanism quota rises to 8% in year two

Boiler manufacturers must now sell heat pumps equal to 8% of their boiler sales or face penalties, which is expected to push manufacturer-backed discounts through the second half of 2026. For households, the practical effect is that headline unit prices should keep drifting down independently of grants.

Source: Grant UK

Warm Homes Plan money starts to move

January's Warm Homes Plan set aside £15bn across schemes, including a larger Boiler Upgrade Scheme allocation and £2bn of low-interest loans for heat pumps, solar and batteries. Zero-rate VAT on all three continues until 31 March 2027, so the combined grant-plus-VAT position is about as favourable as it has been.

Source: The Floor Heating Warehouse

Despite all the attention on lithium, pumped hydro remains by a wide margin the world's dominant form of bulk energy storage.

Developers at this month's Indian pumped hydro conference flagged land acquisition, long build times and offtake structures as the binding constraints on a pipeline that looks enormous on paper. India's storage ambitions increasingly hinge on whether pumped hydro can be built at anything like the speed of batteries.

Bottom line

Storage was the centre of gravity this week: sodium-ion moved decisively from demonstration into mainstream grid procurement, Spain put €165m behind 21 GWh of pumped hydro, and global pumped storage crossed 200 GW for the first time. Nuclear had an unusually good month, with four DOE-authorised microreactors reaching criticality ahead of a public deadline and ITER cooling its first magnet coil without incident. For UK households the practical news is the Boiler Upgrade Scheme uplift to £9,000 for oil and LPG homes, which — stacked with zero-rate VAT and a tightening Clean Heat Market Mechanism — makes this about the most favourable moment yet to leave oil heating behind. The counterweight is solar's soft first quarter in the US and decelerating UK heat pump growth: record annual totals are still coming, but the compound growth rates needed for 2030 targets are slipping. Momentum is real; the trajectory is the thing to keep watching.
