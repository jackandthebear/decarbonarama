☀️ Solar

⚡ Perovskite-silicon tandems hold at a certified 34.85%

LONGi's NREL-certified 34.85% tandem cell remains the benchmark for the technology, achieved via bilayer interface passivation (LiF and EDAI₂) at the perovskite/C₆₀ junction plus resistance-tuned carrier transport layers compatible with textured silicon. It puts tandems comfortably past the single-junction Shockley-Queisser ceiling — the physics case is now settled; the fight is durability and yield.

Source: Green Fuel Journal

Commercial tandem modules ship, but homes wait until 2027–28

Oxford PV's first commercial perovskite tandem panels (24.5% module efficiency) are installed in a US utility-scale project, and California's Tandem PV is closing on 30% at module level with panels currently at 29.7%. Residential availability remains thin — most homeowners buying this year are still buying conventional silicon.

Source: Sunsave

$1.7bn solar-plus-storage breaks ground on a Texas coal site

Panamint Capital has started work on a $1.7 billion solar and storage build at the Calvert coal mine in Texas, sharing the site with the existing coal plant. Separately, Cypress Creek broke ground on a project scaling to 2.5 GW of solar and 2.9 GWh of batteries, and Canadian Solar's CS PowerTech opened phase one of its cell plant in Jeffersonville, Indiana.

Source: Canary Media

🔋 Battery Technology

⚡ Sodium-ion crosses from lab to product line

CATL's TENER sodium energy storage system, launched in Munich, is on track for 1 GWh of cumulative shipments by end-2026, and Changan's Nevo A06 is set to be among the first mass-produced sodium-ion EVs. MIT Technology Review named sodium-ion one of its 10 breakthrough technologies for 2026 — the appeal is no lithium, no cobalt, and better cold-weather behaviour.

Source: MIT Technology Review

Solid-state sodium chemistry targets cheaper, safer grid storage

Researchers have demonstrated a solid-state sodium-ion cell that removes the flammable liquid electrolyte, a combination pitched at both EVs and grid-scale storage. The caveat is scale: solid-state accounts for roughly 1% of existing or funded global battery manufacturing capacity, so this is a 2030s story, not a 2026 one.

Source: Live Science

Capital keeps flowing into battery supply chains

CATL is planning a 40 GWh sodium-ion plant in Fujian alongside a $5bn raise tied to European expansion; GFCL EV raised $130m for advanced battery materials; and RWTH Aachen launched its €50m FastBat cluster across five research centres. The US DOE's $42m EVs4ALL awards target longer life, faster charging and cold-weather range retention.

Source: Battery-Tech Network

♨️ Heat Pumps

Gas boilers out of new UK homes from 2027

The Future Homes Standard bans gas boilers in most new-build homes from 2027, which developers expect to translate into 100,000–300,000 heat pump installations a year. That is a step change from where the market sits today, and puts the pressure squarely on installer capacity.

Source: Thermrite

UK sales at a record — and still only 1% of homes

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme delivered 42,645 installations in 2024 and UK annual heat pump sales reached 98,345 units, the strongest year on record. But roughly 1% of UK homes are heat-pump heated, far behind European peers, with upfront cost and installation disruption still the binding constraints despite the flat £7,500 BUS grant.

Source: Elite Renewables

Industry pushes for regulatory reform to unblock retrofit

Trade bodies are calling for reform to planning, permitted-development and standards rules that slow heat pump retrofit in existing housing stock. The Clean Heat Market Mechanism, requiring 6% of boiler manufacturers' sales to be heat pumps in 2025–26, is the other lever now working through the supply side.

Source: BSEE

Bottom line

The headline of the week is China approving eight reactors in a single State Council sitting — a scale of nuclear commitment no other country is close to matching, and a reminder that policy speed, not technology, is the binding constraint in most markets. Underneath that, the theme is scale-up rather than invention: sodium-ion has left the lab and is shipping into grid storage and EVs, pumped storage has quietly passed 200 GW globally, and perovskite tandems are commercial in utility projects even if they won't reach UK roofs until 2027–28. For British readers the near-term story is the Future Homes Standard — banning gas boilers in new homes from 2027 could pull heat pump volumes from under 100,000 a year towards 300,000, and installer capacity, not consumer demand, will decide whether that lands. Tidal edges towards its first genuine array-scale test at Morlais after two decades of promise. Cheap wind and solar remain the floor under all of it: Lazard again puts them at the bottom of the cost curve even without subsidy.
