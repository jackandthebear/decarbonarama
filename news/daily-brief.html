An EESC co-rapporteur argues that sodium-ion could cut EU dependence on lithium, cobalt and nickel. He calls for an updated battery roadmap and serious scale-up funding. The piece comes as sodium-ion approaches cost parity with LFP.

A Defense Department official told a federal court in Oregon that about 400 onshore wind projects await DOD review, and that eight employees are responsible for them. Plaintiffs say reviews still haven't resumed despite an August injunction, a bottleneck that is holding back US onshore wind.

⚛️ Nuclear

⚡ France launches 10-year national fusion strategy

Minister Philippe Baptiste unveiled the roadmap during a visit to ITER and CEA Cadarache. It builds on ITER, the WEST tokamak and Laser Mégajoule, and aims to have technologies ready for a European pilot plant by 2035, while doubling student numbers in relevant fields.

Source: World Nuclear News

A host community, technology and partner are due "in the coming months."

♨️ Heat Pumps

Bay Area heat pump water heater rule delayed a year after AI-generated pushback

The Bay Area Air District postponed a rule requiring heat pumps when gas water heaters are replaced, after cost objections. At least 85 of the comments were AI-generated, and reporting relayed by the outlet alleges they were linked to a business group with oil-industry funding (unverified). The delay is a setback for one of the most ambitious US building-electrification rules.

Bottom line

Storage is the connecting thread today, from 3.2 GWh paired with Californian solar to nearly a gigawatt-hour at a single Indian site, sodium-ion moving into data-centre power systems, and a 1.9 GW pumped hydro plant coming online in Madhya Pradesh. Nuclear policy is moving on both sides of the Atlantic, with France setting a fusion roadmap and New York shortlisting reactor developers. The headwinds are mainly in the US, where hundreds of onshore wind projects are stuck in Pentagon review and California's heat pump rules have slipped. Ocean energy is still at the pilot stage, but environmental evidence and island-nation interest are both building.
