☀️ Solar

⚡ JinkoSolar pushes perovskite/TOPCon tandem cell past 35.24%

JinkoSolar says a perovskite-on-TOPCon tandem cell has been certified at over 35.24% efficiency by the Chinese Academy of Sciences' Shanghai Institute of Microsystem and Information Technology, beating its own 34.82% mark from five months ago. Stacking perovskite on the TOPCon cells that already dominate production is one of the most likely ways to get past silicon's efficiency limit in mass-market panels. This is a company press release, not independent reporting.

Source: TaiyangNews

⚡ MicroQuanta and Zhejiang University set 23.9% record for a perovskite mini-module

A 19.48 cm² perovskite module reached a certified 23.9% steady-state efficiency with an 83.3% fill factor. The team credits a simple processing tweak: cooling the wet precursor film before vacuum treatment. Module-level records matter more than lab-cell records, because performance usually drops when perovskites are scaled up.

Source: pv magazine

Finnish partners line up 461 MW of utility-scale solar

Solarigo, Wisol and Ensto Maviko have signed a letter of intent to build 461 MW of solar in Finland between 2027 and 2029. Elsewhere in Europe, Alantra Solar raised a €90m debt facility for its Italian platform, and Next2Sun Austria (vertical agri-PV) went into insolvency. Large Nordic solar projects show the technology now pays its way well beyond sunny southern markets.

🔋 Battery Technology

⚡ NW to invest €200m in 1 GWh of distributed battery storage

French developer NW announced at the Choose France summit that it will deploy 400 "JBox" battery units of 1 MW / 2.5 MWh each, about 1 GWh in total, with 300 of them in France. Spreading many mid-sized batteries across the network gives the grid flexibility close to where power is used.

Source: ESS News

L&T wins order worth up to ₹2,500 crore for 900 MWh battery at Khavda

Larsen & Toubro will build a liquid-cooled 300 MW / 900 MWh (three-hour) battery system at Khavda in Gujarat, home to one of the world's largest renewable energy parks. India is quickly adding storage to firm up its huge solar build-out, and Uttar Pradesh's regulator has also just approved 1,500 MWh of tendered batteries.

Source: Business Standard

Sodium-ion closing in on LFP cost parity

A new industry analysis says sodium-ion cells are getting close to the cost of lithium iron phosphate, and supply agreements now cover around 70 GWh. Sodium-ion avoids lithium altogether, so reaching price parity would give grid storage a second chemistry with a more secure supply chain.

Source: pv magazine

💨 Wind

⚡ Green Volt reserves 28 Siemens Gamesa turbines and commits £15m to Aberdeen port

The developers of the Green Volt floating offshore wind farm off north-east Scotland have reserved 28 Siemens Gamesa turbines in what they call a world-first deal, along with a £15 million investment in Aberdeen's port. Securing turbines and port capacity early is one of the biggest hurdles to building floating wind at commercial scale.

Despite October reports that ministers might cut heat pump grants to pay for defence, the Budget kept the Boiler Upgrade Scheme at £7,500 (£9,000 for homes heated by oil or LPG) until 31 March 2027, with £400m of funding for 2027–28. That gives households and installers a clear runway for this winter.

The European Heat Pump Association says residential sales in 12 countries reached about 1.16 million units in the first half of 2026, up from 1.05 million a year earlier. It puts the rebound down to higher oil and gas prices after the Strait of Hormuz closure and to EU plans to cut electricity taxes. (Background item from September.)

💧 Hydro

⚡ US Senate passes hydropower permitting reform

The bill would let operators carry out routine maintenance without FERC approval, create a fast-track licence for hydrokinetic projects of 5 MW or less, and narrow Clean Water Act Section 401 reviews. The National Hydropower Association says 140 licences have already expired and another 300 come up for renewal in the next decade. The bill now goes to the House.

Source: International Water Power

IDOM to engineer Greece's 680 MW Amfilochia pumped storage scheme

IDOM is supporting GEK Terna on the 680 MW (730 MW pumping) Amfilochia project near the Gulf of Arta, covering pump-turbine specifications and powerhouse design. Pumped hydro will help Greece soak up surplus midday solar, which is increasingly being curtailed.

Source: International Water Power
