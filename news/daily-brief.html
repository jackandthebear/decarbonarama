☀️ Solar

⚡ Perovskite-silicon tandems now certified above 34.8%

LONGi's NREL-certified 34.85% tandem cell remains the benchmark as the industry pushes past what was long considered silicon's ceiling. Single-junction perovskite sits at 27.3% certified, and triple-junction lab devices set fresh records this spring — but the cell-to-module gap (Oxford PV modules at 26.9%) is still the commercial bottleneck.

Source: Patsnap / NREL

⚡ Masdar closes finance on 5.2 GW "round-the-clock" solar-plus-storage

Masdar has reached financial close on what it bills as the world's first gigascale round-the-clock renewable project: 5.2 GW of solar paired with 19 GWh of batteries. It is the clearest signal yet that firmed solar — not solar alone — is becoming the unit of utility-scale procurement.

Source: CleanTechnica

New York passes 8 GW of distributed solar

New York State has now installed eight gigawatts of distributed solar, and in June set a record when solar met roughly 29% of statewide demand during the noon hour. Distributed generation at this scale starts to reshape midday wholesale pricing.

Source: Office of the Governor of New York

🔋 Battery Technology

⚡ Peak Energy picks Sacramento for America's first grid-scale sodium-ion plant

Peak Energy selected Sacramento over Texas for the first US grid-scale sodium-ion factory, backed by a $10.5m California Competes tax credit and creating 239 jobs over 18 months. Sodium-ion sidesteps lithium and cobalt entirely — attractive as supply-chain risk climbs up the agenda.

Source: Intelligent Living

Sodium-ion named a 2026 breakthrough technology

MIT Technology Review put sodium-ion on its 10 Breakthrough Technologies list for 2026, reflecting a fast shift from lab curiosity to shipped product. UNIGRID has begun delivering its Na+Casa residential sodium battery into European homes, with US installs expected before year-end.

Source: MIT Technology Review

All-solid-state sodium cells advance at NUS

A National University of Singapore team published an all-solid-state sodium battery design that removes flammable liquid electrolytes altogether. Commercialisation is years away — solid-state is still ~1% of global funded cell capacity — but it points at grid storage that is safer and free of critical minerals.

Source: Yale Environment 360

LFP capacity keeps landing: Morocco, Michigan, Tennessee

The African Development Bank approved €100m for Gotion's Moroccan LFP gigafactory (10 GWh initially, 100 GWh planned), while GM/LG's Ultium Spring Hill line has started making LFP cells for storage and the Ford–CATL Michigan plant began production in June. LFP is quietly becoming the default chemistry for both storage and mass-market EVs.

Source: Fuel Cells Works

💨 Wind

Coastal Virginia hits 81% complete — final turbine slips to end-2027

As of 31 July, America's largest offshore wind farm had 31 of 176 turbines installed and monopile work well advanced, with the project 81% complete overall. First power reached the grid in March; each turbine goes live as it is commissioned, but the final unit is now expected at the end of 2027.

Source: offshoreWIND.biz

First turbine stands at Denmark's largest offshore farm

RWE has installed the first of 72 Siemens Gamesa 15 MW turbines at the 1.1 GW Thor wind farm off Jutland, with all units due in place by the end of 2026. Thor will be the biggest offshore project in Danish waters when complete.

Source: RWE

⚡ Global wind additions hit an all-time high — 169 GW

BloombergNEF reports 169 GW of wind commissioned in 2025, up 38% year-on-year and a third consecutive record. Onshore accounted for 95% of it, with mainland China the first market ever to add more than 100 GW in a single year.

Source: BloombergNEF

⚛️ Nuclear

⚡ Commonwealth Fusion Systems raises another $1bn

CFS announced on 3 August that it has closed a further $1 billion in equity — the largest fusion raise since its own $1.8bn Series B in 2021. The money funds the run-up to SPARC and the follow-on ARC plant in Virginia.

Source: World Nuclear News

Oak Ridge launches FULCRA fusion consortium

Oak Ridge National Laboratory and Rutherford Energy Ventures announced FULCRA on 7 August, a public-private consortium aimed at accelerating fusion scale-up. It is the latest attempt to pull national-lab capability into the private fusion race.

Source: ANS Nuclear Newswire

General Fusion signs Italian deployment deal with Renexia

Canada's General Fusion has agreed a framework with Italian renewables developer Renexia covering siting, funding and construction of one or more magnetized-target fusion plants in Italy. Early-stage, but a rare European commercial siting agreement for fusion.

Source: World Nuclear News

Sweden backs 1.4 GW Rolls-Royce SMR plant

Videberg Kraft is developing a 1.4 GW plant on Sweden's west coast using three Rolls-Royce SMRs, with the Swedish government taking a 60% majority stake from 2027. State equity in SMR projects is becoming the standard route to financing them.

Source: ASME

♨️ Heat Pumps

⚡ Off-gas-grid homes can now claim £9,000

From July 2026 the Boiler Upgrade Scheme pays up to £9,000 for properties off the gas grid — oil and LPG heated homes — against £7,500 for everyone else. Those are exactly the households with the best running-cost case for a heat pump, so the targeting is sensible.

Source: The Floor Heating Warehouse

Warm Homes Plan puts £15bn behind retrofit

January's Warm Homes Plan committed £15bn across schemes, including a bigger BUS allocation and £2bn of low-interest loans covering heat pumps, solar and batteries together. Zero-rated VAT on installs runs to at least March 2027.

Source: Grant UK

Over 180,000 UK installations now government-backed

Cumulative government-supported heat pump installations have passed 180,000, and the Clean Heat Market Mechanism now obliges boiler manufacturers to make heat pumps a set share of sales. Supply-side pressure plus demand-side grants is the combination the sector has been asking for.

Source: pv magazine

💧 Hydro

⚡ Global pumped storage passes 200 GW for the first time

The IHA's World Hydropower Outlook records 28 GW of new hydro in 2025 including a record 11.6 GW of pumped storage, taking the global fleet past 200 GW. Pumped hydro remains far and away the world's dominant form of large-scale storage, with 243 GW under construction.

Source: International Hydropower Association

US Senate approves record FY2026 hydropower funding

The Senate's FY2026 spending package carries the largest federal investment in hydropower and pumped storage since the programme was reinstated in 2008. Long-duration storage is one of the few clean energy lines still attracting bipartisan money.

Source: International Water Power

China's Shangyi station clears two build milestones

The Shangyi pumped storage station in Hebei has closed its lower reservoir for filling and installed the Unit 1 rotor. China continues to account for the bulk of the 621 GW global pumped storage pipeline.

Source: International Water Power

🌊 Wave & Tidal

Canada authorises three Orbital O2-X units at FORCE

Fisheries and Oceans Canada has issued a Fisheries Act authorisation for Eauclaire Tidal to deploy up to three Orbital Marine Power O2-X floating tidal devices at the Bay of Fundy test centre. Regulatory clearance has been the binding constraint at FORCE for years, so this matters.

Source: Ocean Engineering Digital

MeyGen passes 84 GWh cumulative generation

The world's largest operating tidal stream array has now delivered 84 GWh off Scotland's north coast, while Orbital's O2 is running capacity factors above 40% with 90%+ availability. China's "Endeavour" unit has meanwhile clocked 40 months of continuous grid connection.

Source: Offshore Industry

SURF-WEC wave device generating off Oahu

NLR's SURF-WEC wave energy converter, developed with the Hawai'i Marine Energy Center, has been successfully deployed off Oahu and will be the subject of a PRIMRE technical webinar on 11 August. Wave remains a decade behind tidal, but hardware is in the water.

Source: Tethys / PNNL

Bottom line

The theme this weekend is firming: Masdar's 5.2 GW solar plus 19 GWh of batteries, pumped storage crossing 200 GW globally, and record hydro funding in the US Senate all point the same way — generation is no longer the hard part, storing and shaping it is. Sodium-ion is the technology to watch, moving in a single year from lab promise to a US factory site, a residential product shipping in Europe and a spot on MIT's breakthrough list. Fusion had a genuinely big week, with a further $1bn into Commonwealth, a new Oak Ridge consortium and General Fusion's first European siting deal, though none of that produces a kilowatt this decade. For UK homeowners the practical news is the £9,000 off-gas-grid grant now live, aimed squarely at the oil-heated households where the sums work best. Wind, meanwhile, quietly set another global record — 169 GW in 2025 — even as America's flagship offshore project slipped another year.
