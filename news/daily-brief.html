☀️ Solar

⚡ Nanjing team sets meter-scale perovskite record at 22% efficiency

A manufacturing method published in Nature on 12 August by Xiao Ke and Hairen Tan at Nanjing University, with engineers from Renshine, produced 0.72 m² panels delivering 158.4 W of steady output — a certified 22.0% full-area efficiency. That matters because perovskites almost always lose power when scaled from lab chips to real panels; this is the first credible meter-scale result that holds up against mainstream silicon.

Source: Interesting Engineering

US on track for record 43.4 GW of utility-scale solar in 2026

EIA figures have the US adding 43.4 GW of large-scale solar this year, up roughly 60% on 2025 and over half of all new generating capacity. Texas alone accounts for about 40% of the build-out at 17.4 GW, with Arizona and California each near 6%.

Source: PV Tech

Inverted perovskite cell hits 27.17% in certified testing

Chinese researchers earlier this year logged a world-record 27.17% for a single-junction inverted perovskite cell, the architecture most favoured for commercial tandem stacks. Read alongside the Nanjing module result, it shows both the cell physics and the manufacturing story moving at once.

Source: pv magazine

🔋 Battery Technology

⚡ CATL and HyperStrong sign 60 GWh sodium-ion supply deal

The largest sodium-ion storage agreement announced to date covers 60 GWh, using CATL's Naxtra cells rated from −40°C to 70°C. Sodium's appeal for grid storage is cost and cold-weather tolerance rather than energy density, and a deal this size signals the chemistry has left pilot stage for stationary applications.

Source: Battery Tech USA

Tesla's Giga Shanghai builds its 6 millionth battery pack

Tesla confirmed on 18 August that its Shanghai plant has produced six million packs, taking about nine months to go from five to six million. The company reiterated that cell chemistry and pack structure remain in-house, a notable stance as rivals lean harder on external cell suppliers.

Source: CnEVPost

All-solid-state sodium cells target cheaper grid storage

Researchers report a safer all-solid-state sodium battery aimed squarely at stationary storage, sidestepping both lithium supply exposure and the flammability of liquid electrolytes. Companies including UNIGRID are developing sodium solid-state systems for long-life grid duty.

Source: TechXplore

💨 Wind

Inch Cape shifts to turbine installation as components land at Dundee

Foundation components for the Scottish Inch Cape wind farm are arriving at Dundee almost weekly until late September across six vessels, with attention now turning to turbine installation from November. Inch Cape will be the first UK project to use Vestas V236-15.0 MW machines — the largest turbines installed in Britain to date — carried out by Cadeler's new vessel Wind Mover in batches of four.

Source: Energy Global

Revolution Wind past 95% complete with four turbines to go

Ørsted's half-year results confirmed 61 of 65 turbines are installed at Revolution Wind, which will supply Rhode Island and Connecticut. The project is ramping generation and remains on track for commissioning in the second half of 2026.

Source: offshoreWIND.biz

East Anglia THREE completes all 95 monopile foundations

Seaway7's jackup vessel Seaway Ventus has finished its first foundation campaign in the southern North Sea off Suffolk, with every monopile now in place. Foundation completion is the gating item for turbine work, so the project moves into its installation phase on schedule.

Source: reNEWS

♨️ Heat Pumps

⚡ Boiler Upgrade Scheme now pays £9,000 for oil and LPG homes

From 21 July the BUS grant rose to £9,000 where a heat pump replaces heating oil — a £1,500 uplift announced on 26 June and the highest low-carbon heating support available in England and Wales. Off-grid oil and LPG properties now qualify automatically for the top rate, which targets exactly the homes where heat pump economics are strongest.

Source: Aira

EPC requirement dropped and approval times halve

Policy changes this year removed the EPC precondition from BUS applications and cut administrative friction, with average grant approval turnaround roughly halving since 2025. Paperwork, not hardware, has been a persistent brake on UK installs.

Source: Grant UK

Warm Homes Plan and VAT relief shift running-cost maths

The £15bn Warm Homes Plan includes £2bn of low-interest loans for heat pumps, and domestic electricity carries no VAT from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027. Combined with heat pump tariffs from EDF, E.ON Next and British Gas offering cheap off-peak windows, the gas-versus-electricity price spread — the single biggest obstacle to running-cost savings — narrows this winter.

Source: GOV.UK Clean Flexibility Roadmap

💧 Hydro

⚡ Global pumped storage passes 200 GW for the first time

The World Hydropower Outlook reports installed hydropower reached 1,469 GW in 2025 after 28 GW of additions, including a record 11.6 GW of pumped storage. Crossing 200 GW of "water battery" capacity globally underlines that the largest store of grid flexibility on the planet is still gravity, not lithium.

Source: International Hydropower Association

Rye Development advances 2,128 MWh pumped hydro on a Kentucky coal site

The Lewis Ridge project in Bell County would build twin reservoirs on former coal mining land, reusing degraded ground and existing grid connections. Coal-site conversions are becoming the standard playbook for siting long-duration storage in the US.

Source: Energy-Storage.news

Hoover Dam wide-head turbines to restore 160 MW

The Bureau of Reclamation is putting roughly $52m into Hoover Dam, replacing up to three ageing turbines with wide-head units that run at lake elevations below 1,035 ft. Output has fallen about 30% from the dam's 2,074 MW peak as reservoir levels dropped; the retrofit claws back at least 160 MW.

Source: International Water Power

🌊 Wave & Tidal

CorPower C4 holds the first DNV prototype certificate for a wave device

CorPower Ocean's C4, running off Aguçadoura in Portugal since 2023, received the sector's first DNV Prototype Certificate in July. Independent certification is what unlocks insurable, financeable arrays — CorPower is now progressing multi-megawatt deployments in Portugal and at EMEC in Orkney.

Source: Offshore Industry

SURF-WEC deployment lessons shared from Hawaii trials

An 11 August session covered the Small Underwater Research Flap Wave Energy Converter, a 1 m × 1 m oscillating surge device deployed off Oahu. Small-scale devices like this are where survivability data — the perennial killer of wave projects — actually gets generated.

Source: Tethys / PNNL

Tidal stream pipeline the strongest it has ever been

Orbital Marine, SIMEC Atlantis and Nova Innovation are all advancing multi-MW arrays, giving 2026 the most credible near-commercial tidal pipeline in the technology's history. Predictability remains tidal's trump card over wind and solar.

Source: Renewable Energy World

Bottom line

Two themes dominate this morning: scale-up and cheap storage. The Nanjing perovskite result is the first meter-scale panel that survives the jump from lab to factory intact, arriving in the same week the US confirms a record 43 GW solar year — the technology and the deployment curve are finally moving together. On storage, CATL's 60 GWh sodium-ion deal and pumped hydro crossing 200 GW globally point the same direction: the grid is being handed a much cheaper set of flexibility options than lithium alone. For UK readers the heat pump news is the practical headline — £9,000 for oil-heated homes, no EPC hurdle, halved approval times and a VAT holiday on electricity this winter. Offshore wind, meanwhile, is quietly executing: Inch Cape's record-size Vestas machines and East Anglia THREE's completed foundations show the delivery machine still works when the contracts are signed.
