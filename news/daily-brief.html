☀️ Solar

⚡ Solar singled out as US proposes Section 301 tariffs on 14 economies

The US Trade Representative's eight-month probe has proposed tariffs of 15–35% on 14 economies including the EU, China, India and Japan, rising to 20–40% if talks fail. Solar manufacturing is called out specifically in the China, India and Cambodia reports. Comments close 5 January 2027 with hearings on 25 January — a major cost risk for US solar developers relying on imported modules and cells.

Source: PV Tech

The largest is 63.78 MW / 255 MWh at an NHPC-contracted project in Jaisalmer. Pairing solar with storage to deliver firm power is fast becoming India's standard tender model.

🔋 Battery Technology

⚡ Baltics' largest battery completed at Estonian solar park

Evecon and Mirova's joint venture has added a 55 MW / 250 MWh battery to a 77.5 MW solar park near Lihula, western Estonia — billed as the biggest battery in the Baltics by storage capacity. The €110m hybrid park comes with a ten-year flexibility and power purchase agreement with Pure Energy, a first for the region, just as the Baltic states operate independently of the Russian grid.

Source: ESS News

Most BESS suppliers not ready for EU's 2027 Battery Regulation

An Infyos report grading nine system integrators — together more than half of global BESS shipments in H1 2026 — found only 45% have piloted a battery passport and just 13% meet the 2027 due-diligence rules. From 18 February 2027 the passport becomes a condition of EU market access, so supply-chain traceability is about to become a commercial gatekeeper.

Source: Energy-Storage.news

IEEFA proposes pooled "battery reserves" to unlock Australia's grid capacity

An IEEFA report suggests coordinating reserves across many batteries under system integrity protection schemes so underused transmission lines can carry more renewable power. It notes renewable connections in the NEM are running up to eight years ahead of transmission, and calls for modelling, a trial and a new system-operator function.

Source: ESS News

Eku Energy says data centres are competing for the same long-lead transformers and power-conversion kit as battery projects, forcing developers to commit spending earlier. Eku's 250 MW / 500 MWh Williamsdale battery in the ACT recently entered commercial operation.

Source: Energy-Storage.news

Energinet ran a simulated blackout restoration on Zealand in which Kriegers Flak offshore turbines were brought back one by one to help re-energise the grid, after initial power came via the German interconnector. Run with Vattenfall, 50Hertz and EnBW, the test shows wind farms can contribute to "black start" — a role traditionally reserved for fossil and hydro plants.

⚡ NASA cuts lunar reactor target to 20 kW and opens bidding

NASA issued a call on 23 November for Lunar Reactor-1, a 20 kW fission system — a fifth of the 100 kW once promised — that must be launch-ready by 2030 and run five years unattended. Bids are due 21 December. The scale-back reflects the US shortage of HALEU fuel, with domestic supply not expected until around 2029.

💧 Hydro

⚡ US Senate passes hydropower permitting reform bill

The Senate approved a bill letting operators carry out routine maintenance without prior FERC approval, fast-tracking licences for hydrokinetic projects of 5 MW or less, and limiting Clean Water Act 401 reviews to water quality. With 140 licences already expired and about 300 more due this decade, the industry is now pressing the House to act.

Source: International Water Power

A white paper from the International Hydropower Association and the Global Alliance for Pumped Storage, drawing on input from more than 60 governments, sets out seven recommendations to accelerate "water battery" investment, including faster permitting. It frames pumped storage as homegrown, jobs-rich storage to support the COP31 electrification pledge.
