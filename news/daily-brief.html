Global cumulative solar capacity crossed the 3 TW mark this year, a threshold reached barely two years after the second terawatt. The compounding pace matters more than the round number: solar is now adding capacity faster than any generation technology in history, and the constraint has shifted from panels to grids, inverters and storage.

LONGi's NREL-certified 34.85% perovskite-silicon tandem cell remains the benchmark, comfortably above the ~29% Shockley-Queisser ceiling for plain silicon. 🔋 Battery Technology

Announced 3 August, the agreement pairs US startup Mana Battery with France's Saft to develop, validate and commercialise next-generation sodium-ion cells. Saft's industrial credentials give the chemistry a Western manufacturing route at a moment when almost all sodium-ion capacity sits in China.

Source: Sodium Battery Hub

⚡ CATL calls sodium-ion "mainstream-ready" with 15,000-cycle grid cells

CATL is rolling out 5 GWh of its Tener sodium batteries for grid storage, rated for 15,000 cycles — roughly 30 years of daily cycling. It follows April's 60 GWh supply agreement with integrator HyperStrong, the largest sodium order ever placed, and signals the chemistry has moved past pilot scale.

Source: Electrek

Sodium forecast to undercut LFP on cost by 2027

Analysis from Bernstein and Morgan Stanley now has sodium-ion cells beating lithium iron phosphate on cost per kWh as early as next year. Sodium's lower energy density is a non-issue for stationary storage, where footprint matters far less than price and cycle life — which is why grid buyers are moving first.

Source: Sodium Battery Hub

Moment Energy has opened what it describes as the world's largest facility for repurposing retired EV batteries into modular storage for critical infrastructure, targeting 1 GWh of annual output by 2030. Second-life reuse sits upstream of recycling and is the cheapest way to extract remaining value from ageing packs.

Bottom line

Two milestones bookend today's brief: solar past 3 terawatts installed, and pumped hydro past 200 gigawatts. Both are the sort of number that only registers in hindsight, and both point the same way — generation is no longer the hard part, storing and moving it is. Sodium-ion is the story to watch on that front, with CATL declaring it mainstream-ready, Saft entering via a US partnership, and cost parity with LFP now forecast for next year. Nuclear had a genuinely strong 48 hours too, with eight Chinese approvals and TerraPower's first US construction permit in a decade. The nagging counterpoint is wind: order books are up 44% while Vestas and Siemens Gamesa are quietly shelving factories, which is how you get a delivery crunch three years from now.
