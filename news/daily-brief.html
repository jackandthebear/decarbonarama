☀️ Solar

⚡ World passes 3 terawatts of installed solar — largely unremarked

Global cumulative solar capacity crossed the 3 TW mark this year, a threshold reached barely two years after the second terawatt. Solar is now comfortably the fastest-scaling generation technology in history, and the doubling interval keeps shortening.

Source: Energy Connects

Origis brings Rockhound solar close to 1 GW in West Texas

Origis Energy energised three further phases of its Rockhound project in Ector County, adding 500 MWdc and taking the site to roughly 1 GWdc operating. The full build-out, due 2029, is planned to exceed 2 GW of combined solar and storage — a scale increasingly driven by data-centre demand.

Source: Solar Builder

⚡ Perovskite tandem records keep climbing

LONGi now holds an NREL-certified tandem record of 34.85%, with lab tandems crossing 35%, while a Chinese team has set a 28.04% mark for perovskite-organic tandems. Commercial modules remain limited to early utility-scale shipments; mainstream residential availability still looks like a 2027–28 story.

Source: Renewables Now

🔋 Battery Technology

⚡ Saft and Mana Battery sign sodium-ion development deal

The two firms agreed on 3 August to jointly develop, validate and commercialise next-generation sodium-ion cells. It is another sign of an incumbent European cell maker hedging into a chemistry that avoids lithium and cobalt entirely — attractive for stationary storage where energy density matters less than cost.

Source: Sodium Battery Hub

Peak Energy builds America's first grid-scale sodium-ion plant

The Sacramento facility is sized to produce enough annual storage for close to four million homes, with pilot deployments already running in California and New York. Sodium still accounts for only about 4% of global funded cell capacity, so the manufacturing base is the binding constraint rather than the science.

Source: Yale Environment 360

Capital keeps flowing into next-gen cells

Recent rounds include Sila's $300m to scale silicon-carbon anode output at Moses Lake, CATL raising $5bn tied to European expansion plus a planned 40 GWh sodium-ion plant in Fujian, and Tesla committing $250m to lift Giga Berlin cell output toward 18 GWh.

Source: Battery-Tech Network

💨 Wind

Coastal Virginia Offshore Wind hits 81% complete

Dominion's project — the largest offshore wind farm in the US — had 31 turbines installed as of the end of July, with the 32nd underway. The final turbine has slipped to end-2027 from early 2027, though the project has been exporting power to the grid since March.

Source: OffshoreWind.biz

RWE's Thor reaches halfway on turbine installation

Denmark's largest offshore wind farm now has 36 of 72 turbines standing off the Jutland coast. RWE expects the remaining units up by end-2026 and full commissioning by spring 2027.

Source: OffshoreWind.biz

Floating turbine ready to power a UK gas platform

A Vestas turbine on an Ocergy floating foundation has been assembled and is ready for tow to the Culzean field in the North Sea, where it will supply around 20% of the platform's electricity. Platform electrification is emerging as a near-term commercial niche for floating wind while utility-scale costs come down.

Source: OffshoreWind.biz

OEMs stay cautious on factory expansion

Siemens Gamesa has shelved a Danish nacelle plant and Vestas has paused a planned Polish blade factory, reflecting softer-than-expected European offshore demand. Vestas nonetheless posted €3.97bn Q1 revenue, up 14.4%, with orders up 44% year on year — onshore is carrying the business while offshore supply chains reprice.

Source: Breakbulk News

⚛️ Nuclear

⚡ China approves eight more reactors

Beijing has cleared construction of three further pairs of Hualong One (HPR1000) units across multiple sites, continuing an annual approval cadence no other country comes close to matching. Separately, unit 3 at Changjiang in Hainan has begun supplying electricity to the grid.

Source: World Nuclear News

ARC partners with Idaho National Laboratory on SMR deployment

The tie-up announced on 3 August adds to a run of SMR developer–national lab arrangements. Most US SMR programmes still point to initial operation in the early 2030s, with 2026 activity concentrated on permitting, design refinement and supply-chain build-out rather than concrete.

Source: ANS Nuclear Newswire

Fusion's regulatory path clears further

The NRC has formally separated fusion from fission in its licensing rules, and at least three US companies are now seeking permits or building grid-connected plants. The regulatory certainty matters as much as the physics for the financing case.

Source: Earth911

♨️ Heat Pumps

⚡ UK off-gas-grid heat pump grant rises to £9,000

From 21 July 2026 to 31 March 2027, eligible off-gas-grid properties in England and Wales can claim £9,000 towards an air or ground source heat pump — a £1,500 uplift on the standard Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant. The scheme has also dropped the old requirement for an EPC with no outstanding insulation recommendations, removing a common blocker.

Source: Energy Saving Trust

Five New England states launch $450m accelerator

Connecticut, Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire and Rhode Island have co-funded a $450m residential heat pump programme. It is a direct response to the removal of the federal heat pump tax credit from 1 January 2026 — states and utilities are increasingly the policy centre of gravity in the US.

Source: Harvest Thermal

Oregon mandates heat pumps in new homes

From 2026, all new residential buildings in Oregon must install heat pumps rather than conventional air conditioning. With US heat pumps outselling gas furnaces for a second consecutive year, and cold-climate units now rated for reliable operation near −25°C, the technical objections are thinning.

Source: HVAC365

💧 Hydro

⚡ Global pumped storage passes 200 GW for the first time

The IHA's World Hydropower Outlook reports 28 GW of new hydro capacity in 2025 — including a record 11.6 GW of pumped storage — taking the global fleet to 1,469 GW and pumped hydro past 201 GW. It remains by a wide margin the world's dominant form of long-duration storage.

Source: International Hydropower Association

China dominates the construction pipeline

China accounted for over 40% of 2025 capacity additions and now has more than 300 GW of hydropower under construction, of which 218 GW is pumped storage. The global pipeline stands at 1,127 GW including 621 GW of pumped storage.

Source: International Water Power

US Senate approves record FY2026 hydro funding

The FY2026 spending package includes the largest federal investment in hydropower and pumped storage programmes since the programme was reinstated in 2008 — a rare piece of bipartisan clean-energy support in the current US policy climate.

Source: International Water Power

🌊 Wave & Tidal

Morlais tidal site in Wales heads for first turbine deployments

The consented Anglesey site is scheduled for its first turbine installations this year, with capacity for up to 240 MW — enough for more than 180,000 homes at full build. It would be one of the largest tidal stream arrays anywhere.

Source: Offshore Industry

Most credible tidal pipeline yet, but still pre-commercial

Orbital Marine plans six next-generation O2-X turbines in Orkney between 2026 and 2028, with SIMEC Atlantis and Nova Innovation also advancing multi-MW arrays. A pipeline of 16 pre-commercial wave and tidal farms — roughly 196 MW — is expected by 2030, with costs projected to fall toward $120/MWh for wave and $140/MWh for tidal once 2 GW is deployed.

Source: Ocean News

Bottom line

Two milestone numbers land in the same week: solar past 3 terawatts globally, and pumped hydro past 200 gigawatts. Generation and long-duration storage are both scaling, but the interesting movement is in the middle — sodium-ion is moving from lab to factory floor, with Saft's new development deal and Peak Energy's Sacramento plant suggesting the chemistry is close to a real cost position in stationary storage. Policy is fragmenting rather than weakening: the US federal heat pump credit is gone, yet five New England states have replaced it with $450m of their own and Oregon has simply mandated the technology, while the UK has quietly raised its off-gas-grid grant to £9,000. Wind is the outlier, with Siemens Gamesa and Vestas both pausing European factory plans even as installation milestones tick over at Thor and Coastal Virginia — a supply chain repricing rather than a demand collapse. Tidal remains the sector where the pipeline finally looks credible but the first commercial revenue is still ahead.
