☀️ Solar

⚡ US on track for a record 43.4 GW of utility-scale solar in 2026

The EIA's latest outlook has developers adding 43.4 GW of large-scale solar this year — a 60% jump on 2025 and just over half of all new US generating capacity. Texas alone accounts for around 40% of the pipeline, with Arizona, California and Michigan making up most of the rest.

Source: PV Tech

Tehuacana Creek 1 set to be the year's largest new solar plant

The 837 MW Texas project, paired with 418 MW of battery storage, is expected to be the biggest single PV facility energised in 2026. The solar-plus-storage pairing is now close to standard for new US utility projects rather than an add-on.

Source: pv magazine USA

⚡ Perovskite tandems now certified above 34.8%

LONGi holds an NREL-certified record of 34.85% for perovskite-silicon tandem cells, with single-junction perovskite at 27.3%. The gap between lab cells and shipping modules remains wide — commercial tandem panels are still around 24–25% — and long-term field durability is the unresolved question.

Source: Green Fuel Journal

🔋 Battery Technology

⚡ America's first grid-scale sodium-ion plant faces its first big test

Peak Energy's $71m, 183,000 sq ft Sacramento facility will build up to 4 GWh of sodium-ion storage a year from early 2027. It is the first serious test of whether sodium-ion can undercut lithium on cost while sidestepping the fire risk and critical-minerals exposure that dog LFP.

Source: Sodium Battery Hub

Sodium-ion reaches the MISO grid

RWE Americas' Wisconsin installation, energised in March, is the first sodium-ion system backing up the MISO grid, following Peak's 3.5 MWh pilot at Colorado's SolarTAC site. The IEA reports announced global sodium-ion manufacturing pipelines now exceed 400 GWh a year.

Source: Slashdot

All-solid-state sodium cells could cut storage costs further

A National University of Singapore team published an all-solid-state sodium battery in May that removes the flammable liquid electrolyte used in most sodium cells. Solid electrolytes matter most at grid scale, where leakage and thermal runaway drive siting and insurance costs.

Source: TechXplore

US storage developers plan a record 24 GW in 2026

Battery developers have a record year queued up even as new supply-chain content rules bite. LFP continues to take share across vehicle segments, and cylindrical cells are moving upmarket from entry-level EVs into premium platforms.

Source: MIT Technology Review

💨 Wind

All 176 monopiles and transition pieces are installed and 31 turbines are standing at Dominion's 2.6 GW CVOW project, which delivered first power in March. The final turbine is now expected by end-2027, with costs up nearly $300m to about $11.7bn.

Source: offshoreWIND.biz

Siemens Gamesa back in profit as order intake falls

The turbine maker returned to profit in its fiscal Q3 on productivity gains and cost efficiency, offsetting a sharp drop in new orders. Profitability recovering while the order book softens is the sector's defining tension right now.

Source: Windpower Monthly

Shell exits European onshore wind and solar

Shell has agreed to sell its entire European onshore wind and solar portfolio to TotalEnergies — another step in the majors' divergence, with Shell retrenching to hydrocarbons while Total consolidates renewables assets.

Source: Windpower Monthly

CFS has closed the largest fusion equity round since its own $1.8bn Series B in 2021, as it works toward net energy gain on SPARC. The scale of private capital now backing fusion is itself the story — it is no longer a purely public-lab endeavour.

DOE names five states for Nuclear Lifecycle Innovation Campuses

Idaho, Louisiana, Oklahoma, Tennessee and Utah have been selected as potential hosts for campuses covering the full nuclear fuel lifecycle. Fuel-cycle infrastructure is the practical bottleneck for the advanced-reactor fleet the US is trying to build.

The reactor — now the Christopher M. Crane Clean Energy Center — is scheduled to receive nuclear material by year-end, held in storage pending approval to return to service. The restart is underwritten by data-centre demand.

♨️ Heat Pumps

⚡ UK raises off-grid heat pump grants to £9,000

From 21 July 2026 to 31 March 2027, eligible off-gas-grid properties in England and Wales can claim £9,000 under the Boiler Upgrade Scheme, up from the standard £7,500. It targets rural households on oil and LPG, where heat pump economics are strongest but upfront costs bite hardest.

Source: Aira

US shipments hold up despite the 25C credit disappearing

The federal 25C tax credit vanished on 1 January, but monthly air-source heat pump shipments from September 2025 through April 2026 show no clear decline. Heat pumps outsold gas furnaces nationally for a second consecutive year.

Source: The Cool Down

California's SB 222 would streamline heat pump permitting

The reintroduced Heat Pump Access Act aims to modernise permitting statewide to make residential installs faster and cheaper. With federal support withdrawn, states and utilities — Maine, New York, California, Colorado — are now carrying the incentive load.

Source: Canary Media

💧 Hydro

⚡ Global pumped storage passes 200 GW

The IHA's World Hydropower Outlook confirms pumped storage has broken the 200 GW barrier for the first time, with a record 11.6 GW commissioned in a single year out of 28 GW of total hydropower additions. Still the overwhelming majority of the world's stored energy.

Source: International Hydropower Association

Poland signs for 767 MW Rożnów II pumped storage

Poland's Ministry of Energy and Tauron Polska Energia have signed a letter of intent for the 767 MW / 3.1 GWh Rożnów II project — long-duration storage to firm a grid still heavily coal-based.

Source: International Water Power

India commissions first unit of 1 GW variable-speed pumped storage

THDC India, an NTPC subsidiary, has brought the first 250 MW unit online. Variable-speed machines can absorb power while pumping, making them far more useful for balancing solar-heavy grids than fixed-speed units.

Source: POWER Magazine

🌊 Wave & Tidal

⚡ CorPower Ocean wins world-first wave energy certification

DNV has issued the sector's first certification for a wave energy converter, which CorPower frames as the shift from "promising" to "bankable". Certification is what unlocks debt finance and insurance — the practical gate ocean energy has been stuck behind.

Source: Renewable Energy World

Most credible tidal stream pipeline yet

Orbital Marine, SIMEC Atlantis and Nova Innovation are all advancing multi-MW arrays. Orbital's 2 MW O2 at EMEC in Orkney has run since 2021 with capacity factors above 40% and availability over 90% — strong numbers, but the cost curve still lags wind and solar badly.

Source: Offshore Industry

SURF-WEC device deployed off Oahu

A 1m x 1m oscillating surge wave energy converter developed with the Hawai'i Marine Energy Center has been deployed off Oahu, with results presented in a PRIMRE webinar today. Small-scale devices like this target remote and off-grid island loads rather than bulk grid supply.

Source: Tethys / PNNL

Bottom line

The story this morning is storage in all its forms. Sodium-ion crossed from pilot to factory in Sacramento, pumped hydro passed 200 GW globally, and nearly every large US solar project now ships with a battery attached. On the policy side the pattern is inversion: the US has pulled federal heat pump support while shipments hold steady, and the UK has gone the other way, lifting off-grid grants to £9,000 for rural oil and LPG homes — the households with the most to gain. Nuclear's momentum is increasingly private, with Commonwealth Fusion's $1bn round dwarfing most public programmes. And CorPower's DNV certification is the quiet one to watch: wave energy finally has the paperwork that lets lenders take it seriously.
