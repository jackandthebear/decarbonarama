Bottom line

Today's theme is storage cost curves bending in two directions at once: CATL claiming sodium-ion parity with LFP by year-end, and pumped hydro quietly passing 200 GW globally. Between them they answer the "what about when the sun doesn't shine" question from opposite ends — hours versus days. Wind is the split screen: European projects are energising on schedule while the factories that build them sit paused, which stores up a supply problem for the early 2030s. On the UK domestic front, the £9,000 oil-replacement grant plus halved processing times is the most practically useful change for homeowners in months. And fusion keeps raising money faster than it raises plasma — another $1bn to CFS, with ITER, unusually, running ahead of schedule.
