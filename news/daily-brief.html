🔋 Battery Technology

⚡ Sodium-ion approaches cost parity with LFP, says Wood Mackenzie

Supply agreements for sodium-ion cells now exceed 70 GWh, and Wood Mackenzie estimates a 21% lower 20-year total cost of ownership in some storage applications. That would make sodium-ion a credible lithium-free alternative for grid batteries.

Source: ESS News

CATL bets on battery swapping to speed sodium-ion EV adoption

CATL chairman Robin Zeng said a wider swap network will ease range worries that hold back lower-density sodium-ion packs. CATL's stations already cover about 200 Chinese cities, targeting 3,000+ passenger-car stations by year-end.

Source: CnEVPost

Brazil could reach 10 GW of battery storage within five years

Voltxs Energia expects at least 10 GW installed in three to five years, noting more than 300 GW of BESS projects filed with planner EPE ahead of Brazil's first battery capacity auction. Voltxs has registered a 200 MW project for the auction.

💨 Wind

⚡ £12bn Vanguard offshore wind reaches financial close

RWE and KKR closed financing for the 2.8 GW Vanguard East and West projects off Norfolk, backed by 35 banks and £1bn from the UK National Wealth Fund. Offshore construction starts in early 2027 with full commissioning in 2030; SSE also picked Siemens Energy turbines for the 1.4 GW Berwick Bank B.

New England looks to Nova Scotia for offshore wind power

Massachusetts and Nova Scotia launched a request for information on Nova Scotian offshore wind and transmission to New England, with Connecticut, Rhode Island, Vermont and Maine participating. It follows completion of Revolution Wind and Vineyard Wind, the region's first commercial offshore farms.

Source: The Boston Globe

UK Fusion Energy opened five tender lots — covering breeder blanket, fuel cycle, test operations, controls and systems engineering — for the STEP prototype at West Burton. Bids close 29 January 2027; STEP targets operation by 2040.

Details emerge for Canada's National Heat Pump Rebate

The federal program opens via an online portal in early 2027, offering $2,000 rebates, or up to $10,000 for low- to median-income households. Installations from 1 November 2026 qualify retroactively if receipts are filed within six months.
