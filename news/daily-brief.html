Bottom line

Storage was the story this weekend. Quinbrook's A$1.2 billion Supernode, Key Capture's $300 million credit line, Bulgaria adding a gigawatt in a month and pumped hydro crossing 200 GW globally all point the same way: the grid is being rebuilt around the ability to shift energy in time, not just generate it. Solar and wind supply the volume — a record 43.4 GW of US utility solar this year, close to 800 MW of fresh American turbine orders — but it is the batteries and reservoirs that make it dispatchable. On the demand side, heat pumps quietly passed a marker worth noting: US shipments are now running 32% ahead of gas furnaces and European sales are up 25%, meaning the appliance most households will eventually buy is already the one they are buying. The uncomfortable counterpoint came from Europe's nuclear fleet, derated by heat and low river flows again — a reminder that the system we are replacing is not immune to the problem it created.
